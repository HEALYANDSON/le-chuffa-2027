02 · Travel

Getting there

The crossing is included in the price. We'll book it as a group once March 2027 fares are released — either the tunnel or the boat, whichever works out best for the timings.

Option A

Eurotunnel Le Shuttle

Folkestone → Calais in 35 minutes, drive on and stay in the car. The quickest way over and the easiest with clubs in the boot.

Le Touquet is around 1 hour 10 from the Calais terminal via the A16.

Option B

Ferry · Dover–Calais

DFDS or P&O, 90 minutes on the water. More sailings to choose from, a bar on board, and usually the cheaper option for a car of four.

Same drive the other side — roughly 70 km south to the coast.

Cars. We'll organise car shares so every vehicle has four in it — one crossing fare per car keeps the cost down. Say when you pay your deposit if you're happy to drive.
03 · Courses

Two rounds on the Côte d'Opale

Pines, sand and sea air. This is the stretch of coast the British built golf on in the 1920s and '30s, and it still plays like it.

Friday 26 March · First tee 10:20

La Mer

Le Touquet Golf Resort · Harry Colt & C.H. Alison, 1931

Billed as the first links in France. An out-and-back routing through proper sand dunes, restored in 2015 to strip out the trees that had crept in over the decades and give it back its open, windswept character. Slick run-offs, deep bunkering around the greens and a view of the Channel from the 18th tee.

Ranked 12th in France and inside the top 60 in Continental Europe.

Les Dunes

Golf d'Hardelot · opened 1990

Twenty-five minutes up the coast at Hardelot, tucked into the undulating dunes of Mont Saint-Frieux. Tighter and more tumbling than La Mer, with blind shots and doglegs through the pines — a course that rewards the man who plays it in his head before he plays it off the tee.

Ranked 50th in France. Conveniently on the way back north to Calais for the Saturday crossing.

04 · Stay

Le Manoir Hotel

Two nights on the golf course itself. Wake up, walk to the range.

The founder's house

4-star · Le Touquet Golf Resort · 2170 avenue du Golf

Built in 1911 as the private home of Allen Stoneham, the Englishman who founded Golf du Touquet, and turned into a boutique hotel in 2017. Anglo-Norman on the outside — stone, turret, red shutters — and a warm, wood-panelled lounge on the inside. Forty-one rooms, so we'll have most of the place.

La Table du Manoir restaurant and Le M Bar are downstairs; Le Touquet town and the seafront are a few minutes' drive.
